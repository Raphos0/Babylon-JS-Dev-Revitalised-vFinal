Professionalism in the Games Dev Industry, What does it mean?

To a lot of people, professionalism may appear to be a foreign concept in a world of creative design – a limiter to what a creative mind can do simply to adhere to a company policy. However, today I write to discuss why this is not the case, and in fact, why it is beneficial and essential to uphold professional standards and basic codes of conduct in order to create a good game, especially within larger dev teams.



The basic definition of professionalism, referencing the Cambridge Dictionary, is:


“The combination of all the qualities that are connected with trained and skilled people”


Though, what does this mean? Well, even this definition may vary – there are a lot of things that have to be considered when referring to the “qualities” that are essential to upholding a professional practice – this can include knowledge, skills, behaviour, ethics and adaptability. In general, professionals are regarded as people who have a level of expertise above the average person, but also a person with higher standards – “professionals have standards”, as the saying goes. I believe that it is this idea of expectations and high standards which leads people to think that professionalism only ruins the workplace of Games Development. However, Games Dev is still a business and there are sure to be people that have different motivations behind the reason they are within the industry -whether that be passion or paychecks- but it is basic courtesy to practices the codes of conduct for this reason, as there will always be people that you may personally disagree with, but the work is required to be done regardless.


A better way to view professionalism is to look at the pillars of it’s structure, which are Commitment, Integrity, Responsibility and Accountability, and how they apply in the context of the games industry:



- Integrity: A part of professionalism comes with obfuscating the truth or your personal opinions, as to not hurt the relationship between you and your co-worker(s). However, too often are there miscommunications between dev teams, especially in big companies, due to a lack of clear ideas or the feedback to refine them - which leads to a mediocre product. A good dev team is one that can effectively communicate their ideas and be willing to accept feedback at the same time, no matter the position. Additionally, dev teams may be of different disipline areas, however it is proper practice to give suggestions where necessary when something needs to be addressed, and the receiving end should keep an open mind to suggestions outside of their disipline area, as it may allow them to think outside of the box and lead to a better product.



- Responsibility: Professionalism does not only include how you conduct yourself in the workplace, but also you skillset and experience. It should be your basic responsibility to look out for your co-workers, and professional would not only utilise their skillset and experience for themselves, but for other people in the workspace too. Helping your co-workers and junior employees with tasks they’re not confident in doing by giving a pointer or two can go a long way with increasing productivity and building a healthier working environment.




- Accountability: The act of accountability is taking responsbility for your mistakes and make amends for it. This can be called for cause in a variety of reasons but it is important to uphold this code of conduct and take accountability for your mistakes, as to not let resentment build up in the workplace. Though, in the common case of a miscommunication between dev teams, I believe it is important to not simply blame one party or the other, but to put differences aside and take action together in order to remedy the situation. The larger the resulting problem, the more important it is to do this in order to not waste time on development and upholding the reputation of the company by delivering a good game despite the mistakes along the way.



- Commitment: Not every decision gets to be made by a single dev team, as there will always be a Creative Director that will dictate the general flow of the project Game Directors and people in other fields of development will make decisions you will have to accept, whether it clashes with design decisions in your field of development or turns something completely on its head. However, you can still commit yourself to give the feedback which you believe needs to be addressed or simply hear out the reasoning behind the decision that has been made – it’s important to make the most of this decision and take creative liberties in some cases, rather than directly combating it.



In conclusion, Professionalism isn’t simply the expanse of your skillset or a limiting factor in the creative process, it’s an enhancer to it – having a proper workflow with respect and understanding between dev teams or even individual developers. Upholding these principles of responsibility, integrity, accountability and commitment while understanding your workspace environment as you do it through utilising your skillset to help your other co-workers, being willing to be flexible during the development process -whether that be questionable decisions or otherwise- will make you gain an equal amount of respect from both parties and will lead to a healthier and more productive working environment for everybody included.
